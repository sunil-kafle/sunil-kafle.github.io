Climate-Smart Agriculture: Seasonal NDVI Monitoring

A multi-year vegetation monitoring project using Sentinel-2 imagery, Google Earth Engine, and Python-based geospatial analysis to track seasonal NDVI patterns in Tippecanoe County, Indiana (2018–2024).

Overview

This project analyzes how vegetation conditions changed across growing seasons in Tippecanoe County, Indiana using Sentinel-2 surface reflectance imagery. Each year is summarized using the same seasonal window (May 1 – September 30) to enable consistent year-to-year comparison.

Rather than relying on a single visual map, the workflow generates structured raster and tabular outputs that allow for systematic comparison of seasonal vegetation strength across multiple years.

Approach

The workflow begins in Google Earth Engine, where Sentinel-2 imagery is filtered by county boundary, season, and cloud conditions. Seasonal median composites are generated and NDVI is calculated from red and near-infrared bands.

Exported GeoTIFF rasters are processed locally in Python for summary statistics, classification, and grid-based aggregation. Annual NDVI values are converted into structured CSV datasets to support multi-year comparison and visualization.

Key Outputs

  • Seasonal NDVI rasters (2018–2024)
  • Vegetation classification maps
  • Grid-level NDVI datasets for each year
  • Multi-year comparison tables and figures

Main Findings

The analysis shows clear year-to-year variation in vegetation conditions. 2024 recorded the strongest seasonal NDVI levels, while 2021 showed the weakest. The largest negative change occurred between 2020 and 2021, and the decline was spatially widespread rather than localized.

The results demonstrate that vegetation conditions fluctuate across seasons rather than following a simple linear trend.

Tools & Technologies

Python, Google Earth Engine, Sentinel-2 Surface Reflectance, Raster Processing, Geospatial Analysis, Pandas, Matplotlib
